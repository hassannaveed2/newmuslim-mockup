Raised Baptist Atlanta, USA Muslim since 2016

“My grandmother’s Bible is still on my table. It’s where my questions began.”

At 58, Khadija sat down with a Quran to prove her grandson wrong. Three years later she was teaching the Sunday class at her local masjid.

Khadija Freeman

My grandson Marcus came home from his first year of college and told me, very politely, that he had become a Muslim. I smiled, I hugged him, and then I went into the kitchen and said to myself: Lord, give me the words to bring this boy home.

I had taught Sunday school for twenty-two years. I knew my Bible. I figured I would read his Quran, find the places where it went wrong, and we would have a good long talk over dinner. That was the plan.

The bet with my grandson

Marcus didn’t argue with me. He just said, “Grandma, read it like you’d read anything you love. Slowly.” So I did. I kept it next to my grandmother’s Bible on the kitchen table — that worn black book with her handwriting in the margins — and I read them side by side every morning with my coffee.

I was looking for mistakes. What I kept finding were the people I already loved: Abraham, Moses, Noah, Mary.

I opened it to prove a point. I kept reading because I recognised the voice.

Finding Mary in the Quran

Nobody had ever told me there is a whole chapter named after Mary — Surah Maryam, the nineteenth. I read it three times that first night.

The way it spoke about her, with such tenderness and honour, undid something in me. I had expected an enemy. I found a family I didn’t know I had.

The first time I prayed

I took my shahada on a Tuesday afternoon in the spring of 2016, at a small masjid off Memorial Drive. Marcus was beside me. So was a sister named Halima, who had driven forty minutes just to hold my hand.

My knees are not what they were, and I was embarrassed to pray sitting in a chair. Halima laughed and pulled up a chair next to mine. “Allah knows your knees, Auntie,” she said. I have never felt so welcome anywhere in my life.

Telling my church family

That was the hardest part. Some friends stopped calling. Others surprised me. My pastor came to the house, sat at that same kitchen table, and we talked for three hours about the Prophet Jesus, peace be upon him, and about the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ. We didn’t agree on everything. We still have lunch every month.

What I’d tell you

If you are reading this because someone you love has become Muslim — read what they’re reading. Slowly. With love. And if you are reading this because you’re the one with the questions, I promise you: you are not too old, too late, or too far away. I was 58.

My grandmother’s Bible is still on my table. Next to it is my Quran. Both of them taught me to look for God. One of them showed me where He had been all along.
